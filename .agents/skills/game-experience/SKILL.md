name: game-experience
description: Design or revise a game's player experience, progression, UI, help and Japanese copy from a brief or play feedback; connect intended actions to observable results and evaluate understanding and enjoyment separately.
---

Start from the user's desired play and the current concrete difficulty. Keep the
chosen genre and scope; Red Dune is a case, not a generation template. Reuse the
brief and feedback rather than asking a fixed questionnaire.

Read only the reference needed for the next decision:

- For motivation, a playable slice, later play or interpreting feedback, read
  [遊びの設計](../../../docs/game-experience/design.md).
- For screens, controls, help or labels, read
  [画面と日本語](../../../docs/game-experience/ui-and-japanese.md).
  Read its Japanese-writing section before drafting substantial Japanese copy.
- When checking a claim's origin, scope or confidence, read
  [出典と検証範囲](../../../docs/game-experience/sources.md).

Apply the relevant judgment to an actual interaction: what the player can try,
what really changes, and how they can notice it. Fix the most consequential gap
and play the changed path. Do not turn the references into compulsory scorecards
or substitute a research report for requested game work.

Distinguish theory, developer accounts, implementation/replay observations,
human feedback and untested hypotheses. A correct replay is not human enjoyment;
one positive trial is not evidence of durable appeal or transfer to every genre.
Report only the evidence needed for the current decision and its remaining limit.

In the foundation clone, use `$new-game` for a new workspace and `$fp-gamedev`
for implementation. In an independent game, follow its local continuation entry.
Keep this entry and its three linked documents reachable in that game's local
continuation guide, by copying the bundle with the foundation license/revision
or linking to a reviewed public commit. Do not depend on private memory.
